# Purpose

This document defines the official framework for documenting, evaluating, maintaining, and governing design decisions throughout the evolution of Omon Dashboard.

Rather than prescribing visual solutions or documenting individual design choices, this document establishes a standardized decision-making system that enables consistent product evolution over time.

Its primary objective is to ensure that every significant design decision can be understood, reviewed, traced, and revisited without relying on personal memory or historical context.

The Design Decision framework promotes long-term consistency across the entire Product Experience Blueprint while reducing ambiguity during future iterations.

This document serves as the single governance reference for strategic Product Experience decisions.

# 1. Introduction

Every product evolves.

As new user needs emerge, technologies advance, business priorities shift, and product capabilities expand, the Product Experience must continuously adapt without losing its identity.

Design evolution is therefore inevitable.

However, unmanaged evolution often introduces inconsistency.

Without a structured approach to documenting why a particular design direction was chosen, future teams may unintentionally reverse previous decisions, duplicate discussions, or introduce conflicting experiences across different parts of the product.

The purpose of this document is to prevent that situation.

Rather than documenting visual specifications or implementation details, this document establishes the governance framework for recording strategic Product Experience decisions throughout the lifecycle of Omon Dashboard.

A Design Decision represents a documented conclusion reached after careful evaluation of available alternatives, aligned with the Product Experience Blueprint, and intended to guide future evolution of the product.

Documenting decisions provides long-term continuity beyond individual contributors, project phases, or redesign initiatives.

Instead of relying on institutional memory, every significant Product Experience decision should become part of an organized knowledge base that explains not only what was decided, but also why the decision exists and under which assumptions it was made.

This framework promotes transparency, consistency, accountability, and long-term maintainability across the entire design ecosystem.

The Design Decision framework complements the Product Experience Blueprint by ensuring that future changes remain intentional rather than accidental.

It enables Omon to evolve confidently while preserving its core identity as a Calm Financial Companion.

# 2. Purpose of Design Decisions

The objective of documenting design decisions is not to archive discussions.

Its objective is to preserve reasoning.

Design artifacts such as mockups, prototypes, and specifications describe what a product should become.

A Design Decision explains why that direction was selected.

Separating rationale from implementation creates a stronger foundation for sustainable product evolution.

Every documented decision should contribute to one or more long-term objectives.

## Preserve Design Intent

Product interfaces naturally change over time.

Without documented intent, future redesign efforts risk solving problems that were already considered or introducing solutions that contradict established Product Experience principles.

Capturing the intent behind a decision preserves its original purpose beyond visual execution.

---

## Reduce Repetitive Discussions

Many strategic design topics inevitably reappear during product growth.

A documented decision allows future teams to reference existing reasoning before reopening the same conversation.

This reduces unnecessary debate while improving organizational efficiency.

---

## Improve Consistency

Consistency is achieved not only through reusable components but also through consistent reasoning.

When similar situations are evaluated using comparable principles, the resulting Product Experience becomes more predictable and coherent.

Documented decisions provide that consistency across time.

---

## Support Transparent Decision Making

Design choices should never appear arbitrary.

Recording the context, objectives, assumptions, evaluation criteria, and expected outcomes creates transparency for everyone involved in the product lifecycle.

Transparency improves collaboration between design, product, engineering, and business stakeholders.

---

## Enable Future Re-evaluation

No design decision should be considered permanently correct.

Every decision reflects the best available understanding at a particular moment.

As new evidence becomes available, documented reasoning enables future teams to evaluate whether assumptions remain valid.

This approach encourages continuous improvement while avoiding unnecessary redesign.

---

## Protect Product Identity

Rapid product growth often introduces fragmentation.

Individual improvements may appear beneficial in isolation while gradually weakening the overall Product Experience.

Documented decisions act as safeguards that continually reconnect future work with the principles established throughout the Product Experience Blueprint.

This ensures that every evolution continues supporting Omon's positioning as a Calm Financial Companion.

---

## Build Organizational Knowledge

Design knowledge should become a durable organizational asset rather than remaining within individual experience.

Documented decisions gradually create an institutional memory that accelerates onboarding, improves cross-functional understanding, and reduces dependency on specific individuals.

As the product matures, this knowledge base becomes increasingly valuable for maintaining long-term Product Experience quality.

---

The Design Decision framework therefore serves not as documentation for documentation's sake, but as a strategic governance mechanism that enables Omon to evolve deliberately, consistently, and sustainably while preserving the principles established throughout the Product Experience Blueprint.
# 3. Design Decision Philosophy

Design decisions are long-term commitments rather than temporary preferences.

Every significant Product Experience decision should be made deliberately, documented transparently, and maintained responsibly throughout the product lifecycle.

Within Omon Dashboard, a design decision is not considered successful merely because it solves an immediate problem.

A successful decision continues providing value as the product evolves, scales, and adapts to future requirements.

For this reason, decisions should always prioritize enduring product quality over short-term convenience.

The Design Decision framework is founded on the belief that consistency emerges from disciplined reasoning rather than repeated visual patterns alone.

Two interfaces may appear visually similar while being driven by entirely different reasoning.

Likewise, two interfaces may look different yet remain fully consistent because both are derived from the same Product Experience principles.

The objective of documenting decisions is therefore to preserve the reasoning process instead of preserving a particular implementation.

Every documented decision should answer questions that remain meaningful long after the original implementation has changed.

Examples include:

- What problem was being solved?
- Why was this approach selected?
- Which alternatives were considered?
- Which Product Experience principles influenced the decision?
- Under what assumptions was the decision made?
- Under which circumstances should the decision be reconsidered?

By preserving reasoning instead of implementation, the Product Experience Blueprint remains resilient against technological change, organizational growth, and future redesign initiatives.

Design decisions should also be viewed as evolving knowledge.

A decision represents the best conclusion based on currently available information.

It is never treated as immutable doctrine.

As user behavior, product strategy, technology, accessibility standards, or organizational priorities evolve, previously documented decisions may also evolve through a structured governance process.

The Design Decision framework therefore balances two equally important goals:

- Preserve long-term consistency.
- Enable responsible evolution.

Neither objective should exist without the other.

# 4. Decision Principles

Every strategic Product Experience decision should follow a consistent set of principles regardless of the feature, interface, or project phase.

These principles establish a shared foundation for evaluating future decisions and maintaining alignment across the Product Experience Blueprint.

## Principle 1 — User-Centered Reasoning

Every decision should begin by understanding user needs rather than interface preferences.

The primary objective is improving the user's overall experience instead of satisfying subjective aesthetic opinions.

User value remains the first consideration throughout the decision-making process.

---

## Principle 2 — Alignment with Product Philosophy

Every decision should reinforce the Product Positioning, Brand Identity, Design Philosophy, and Product Experience defined throughout the blueprint.

A technically valid solution may still be rejected if it weakens the intended product identity.

Consistency with the established philosophy takes precedence over isolated optimization.

---

## Principle 3 — Evidence Before Preference

Whenever possible, strategic decisions should be supported by observable evidence rather than personal opinion.

Evidence may originate from research, usability findings, accessibility evaluation, heuristic analysis, design audit results, product objectives, or other structured evaluation methods.

Personal preference alone should never become sufficient justification for a strategic Product Experience decision.

---

## Principle 4 — Simplicity Over Complexity

When multiple solutions achieve comparable outcomes, preference should be given to the solution that minimizes unnecessary complexity.

Simplicity improves usability, maintainability, learnability, and long-term consistency.

Complexity should exist only when it clearly creates proportional value.

---

## Principle 5 — Consistency Before Novelty

Innovation is valuable when it improves the Product Experience.

However, novelty should never compromise consistency across the product.

New ideas should extend the existing Product Experience rather than fragment it.

Users benefit more from predictable interactions than from isolated moments of visual uniqueness.

---

## Principle 6 — Explicit Decision Making

Significant Product Experience changes should never rely on implicit assumptions.

Major decisions should be consciously documented with sufficient context, rationale, expected outcomes, and review criteria.

Transparency improves accountability while reducing future ambiguity.

---

## Principle 7 — Reversible Whenever Possible

Not every decision needs to become permanent.

Whenever practical, decisions should be structured so they can be evaluated and revised without introducing unnecessary disruption to the overall Product Experience.

Design flexibility supports sustainable product evolution.

---

## Principle 8 — Long-Term Maintainability

A decision should remain understandable months or years after its creation.

Future contributors should be able to understand its purpose without depending on the original decision makers.

Documentation therefore becomes part of the design itself rather than an afterthought.

---

## Principle 9 — Cross-Functional Understanding

Design decisions affect more than designers.

Product managers, developers, quality assurance, researchers, and stakeholders should all be able to understand the intent behind documented decisions.

Documentation should therefore prioritize clarity over specialized terminology.

---

## Principle 10 — Continuous Improvement

Every documented decision remains open to future evaluation.

Learning from user feedback, product evolution, changing requirements, and accumulated experience should strengthen future decisions rather than invalidate previous work.

Continuous improvement is considered a characteristic of a healthy Product Experience rather than an indication of earlier failure.

---

Collectively, these principles provide the foundation for every future decision recorded within the Design Decision framework.

Regardless of how the product evolves, the reasoning process should remain consistent, transparent, user-centered, and aligned with the long-term vision established throughout the Product Experience Blueprint.
# 5. Decision Lifecycle

Every strategic Product Experience decision follows a structured lifecycle.

Rather than appearing as isolated events, decisions are treated as living assets that evolve alongside the product.

A structured lifecycle promotes transparency, consistency, accountability, and long-term maintainability while reducing the risk of undocumented or conflicting design changes.

Although individual decisions may vary in scope and complexity, every decision should progress through the same conceptual stages.

---

## Stage 1 — Identify

Every decision begins with identifying a meaningful Product Experience need.

A decision should originate from a clearly understood problem, opportunity, observation, or organizational requirement rather than from implementation convenience or individual preference.

The objective of this stage is to establish that a decision is actually required.

Typical triggers may include:

- New Product Experience requirements.
- Identified usability issues.
- Accessibility improvements.
- Consistency gaps.
- Product evolution.
- Brand alignment.
- User research findings.
- Audit outcomes.
- Organizational learning.

At this stage, no solution has been selected.

Only the need for a decision has been established.

---

## Stage 2 — Understand

Before evaluating possible solutions, sufficient context should be gathered.

The purpose of this stage is understanding the problem rather than immediately attempting to solve it.

Relevant context may include:

- User needs.
- Product goals.
- Existing blueprint guidance.
- Design constraints.
- Historical context.
- Previous decisions.
- Available evidence.
- Known assumptions.

A well-understood problem often leads to significantly better decisions than a quickly selected solution.

---

## Stage 3 — Explore

Alternative approaches should be explored before selecting a direction.

The objective is not to maximize the number of options but to ensure that multiple reasonable perspectives have been considered.

Alternative exploration encourages critical thinking while reducing confirmation bias.

Each alternative should be evaluated according to the principles established within this blueprint.

Rejected alternatives remain valuable because they document reasoning that may become useful in future evaluations.

---

## Stage 4 — Decide

Once sufficient understanding has been established, a preferred direction may be selected.

A documented decision should clearly communicate:

- The selected direction.
- The reasoning behind the decision.
- Expected benefits.
- Known trade-offs.
- Relevant assumptions.
- Expected impact.
- Conditions that may require future review.

The objective is not merely recording the conclusion.

The objective is preserving the reasoning that produced the conclusion.

---

## Stage 5 — Document

The selected decision should be formally documented using the standards defined within this framework.

Documentation transforms temporary knowledge into organizational knowledge.

A documented decision should remain understandable without requiring verbal explanation from the original contributors.

Documentation should emphasize clarity, traceability, and future maintainability.

---

## Stage 6 — Apply

Once documented, the decision becomes part of the Product Experience governance framework.

Future design activities should align with documented decisions unless an approved revision supersedes them.

Application does not imply rigid enforcement.

Instead, it establishes a shared reference that guides future design work toward consistent outcomes.

---

## Stage 7 — Review

Every decision should remain open to future evaluation.

As new information becomes available, assumptions may change.

User behavior evolves.

Business priorities shift.

Technology advances.

Accessibility standards improve.

The review stage verifies whether the original reasoning continues supporting the intended Product Experience.

Review focuses on validating reasoning rather than searching for mistakes.

---

## Stage 8 — Revise or Retire

Some decisions continue providing long-term value.

Others eventually require revision.

In some situations, a decision may no longer be relevant because the surrounding product context has fundamentally changed.

When this occurs, the previous decision should never simply disappear.

Instead, it should be formally superseded, revised, or retired while preserving its historical traceability.

Maintaining historical context prevents future teams from repeating previously resolved discussions.

---

## Lifecycle Characteristics

Regardless of decision complexity, the lifecycle should always exhibit the following characteristics:

- Transparent.
- Repeatable.
- Evidence-informed.
- Traceable.
- Reviewable.
- Maintainable.
- Adaptable.
- Independent of implementation technology.

The lifecycle exists to ensure that design reasoning evolves with the product while remaining understandable across multiple generations of contributors.

# 6. Decision Ownership

Strategic Product Experience decisions require clear ownership.

Ownership establishes accountability for maintaining the quality, consistency, and long-term integrity of documented decisions.

Ownership does not imply exclusive authority.

Instead, it defines responsibility for ensuring that decisions are properly evaluated, documented, reviewed, and maintained throughout their lifecycle.

The Design Decision framework encourages collaborative decision making while maintaining clear accountability.

---

## Ownership Principles

Decision ownership should always prioritize the long-term health of the Product Experience rather than individual preferences or organizational hierarchy.

Ownership exists to protect product consistency rather than personal authority.

Every owner should act as a steward of the Product Experience Blueprint.

---

## Responsibilities of Decision Owners

A decision owner is responsible for ensuring that each documented decision:

- Addresses a clearly defined problem.
- Aligns with the Product Experience Blueprint.
- Follows the documented decision process.
- Includes sufficient supporting rationale.
- Records relevant assumptions.
- Documents expected outcomes.
- Defines future review conditions.
- Remains understandable over time.

Ownership therefore extends beyond creating documentation.

It includes maintaining documentation throughout the decision lifecycle.

---

## Shared Ownership

Some Product Experience decisions naturally span multiple disciplines.

In such situations, ownership should be collaborative while remaining clearly coordinated.

Shared ownership encourages diverse perspectives without sacrificing accountability.

Every contributor should understand:

- Their area of responsibility.
- Their contribution to the evaluation process.
- Their role during future reviews.
- Their responsibility for maintaining decision quality.

Collaboration strengthens decision quality by incorporating multiple perspectives while preserving a single documented source of truth.

---

## Ownership Continuity

Design decisions should outlive individual contributors.

If responsibilities change over time, ownership should transition without losing historical context.

Documentation should therefore remain sufficiently complete for future owners to understand:

- Why the decision exists.
- Which assumptions influenced it.
- Which alternatives were considered.
- Which outcomes were expected.
- Under what conditions the decision should be reviewed.

Ownership continuity protects the long-term integrity of the Product Experience regardless of organizational changes.

---

## Accountability Without Rigidity

Ownership provides accountability but should never discourage constructive discussion.

Healthy Product Experience governance encourages questioning assumptions, proposing improvements, and revisiting earlier conclusions when supported by appropriate evidence.

Documented ownership creates responsibility.

It does not create immunity from future improvement.

The ultimate objective is preserving the quality of the Product Experience rather than preserving individual decisions indefinitely.
# 7. Decision Documentation Standard

The long-term value of a design decision depends not only on the quality of the decision itself, but also on the quality of its documentation.

A well-reasoned decision that is poorly documented gradually loses its usefulness as organizational knowledge fades over time.

The purpose of documentation is therefore to preserve understanding rather than merely record outcomes.

Every documented decision should remain understandable regardless of when it is reviewed or who reviews it.

Documentation should prioritize clarity, context, and reasoning over excessive detail.

The objective is enabling future contributors to understand why a decision exists without requiring additional explanation from its original authors.

---

## Documentation Principles

Every documented decision should exhibit the following characteristics.

### Clear

The purpose of the decision should be immediately understandable.

Readers should not need to infer the intent behind the documented reasoning.

Ambiguity should be minimized throughout the documentation.

---

### Contextual

Every decision exists within a broader Product Experience context.

Documentation should explain the circumstances that led to the decision rather than presenting the conclusion in isolation.

Understanding context improves future evaluation and prevents incorrect interpretation.

---

### Reasoned

Documentation should explain why a particular direction was selected.

The emphasis should remain on reasoning rather than implementation details.

Reasoning provides lasting value even after implementation changes.

---

### Evidence-Informed

Whenever applicable, documentation should reference the observations, research, evaluation methods, audit findings, usability insights, accessibility considerations, or strategic objectives that influenced the decision.

Evidence increases confidence while reducing subjective interpretation.

---

### Traceable

Every documented decision should be discoverable within the broader Product Experience governance system.

Future contributors should be able to identify relationships between decisions, supporting documents, and subsequent revisions.

Traceability preserves organizational learning.

---

### Maintainable

Documentation should remain useful throughout the lifecycle of the product.

As decisions evolve, documentation should continue reflecting their current status while preserving historical context.

Maintainability is an ongoing responsibility rather than a one-time activity.

---

## Documentation Quality Expectations

High-quality documentation should answer questions such as:

- What challenge required a decision?
- Why was a decision necessary?
- Which reasoning influenced the selected direction?
- Which assumptions supported the decision?
- What outcomes were expected?
- Under what conditions should the decision be reviewed?

These questions guide documentation quality rather than acting as a mandatory template.

Different decisions may require different levels of detail depending on their scope and strategic importance.

---

## Documentation Consistency

Consistency across documented decisions is essential for long-term governance.

Although the structure of individual records may evolve over time, the quality expectations established within this framework should remain stable.

Consistent documentation improves discoverability, comparison, review, onboarding, and future decision making.

The documentation standard therefore exists to preserve organizational understanding rather than enforce rigid formatting rules.

---

# 8. Decision Classification

Not every Product Experience decision carries the same strategic significance.

Some decisions influence the overall direction of the product, while others affect only limited aspects of the user experience.

Classifying decisions according to their strategic impact allows governance efforts to remain proportional to the importance of each decision.

Classification supports prioritization without implying that lower-impact decisions are less valuable.

Every documented decision contributes to the overall quality of the Product Experience.

---

## Classification Objectives

Decision classification serves several purposes:

- Establish proportional governance.
- Support appropriate review depth.
- Improve documentation consistency.
- Prioritize long-term maintenance.
- Clarify expected organizational impact.
- Improve traceability across the Product Experience Blueprint.

Classification should simplify governance rather than increase administrative overhead.

---

## Strategic Decisions

Strategic decisions influence the long-term direction of the Product Experience.

They often affect multiple blueprint documents, shape future design principles, or establish enduring product-wide guidance.

Strategic decisions typically require broader evaluation, stronger supporting rationale, and more comprehensive long-term governance.

Because of their wide-reaching impact, changes to strategic decisions should be approached with particular care.

---

## Tactical Decisions

Tactical decisions refine existing Product Experience direction without fundamentally altering the underlying philosophy.

They generally improve consistency, usability, clarity, or maintainability within an established strategic framework.

Although tactical decisions may have meaningful product impact, they normally operate within previously approved principles rather than redefining them.

---

## Operational Decisions

Operational decisions address localized Product Experience improvements that remain fully aligned with existing design guidance.

These decisions generally have limited scope and minimal long-term strategic consequences.

Operational decisions still benefit from structured documentation, but governance activities should remain proportional to their impact.

---

## Classification Characteristics

Decision classification should always consider factors such as:

- Expected Product Experience impact.
- Breadth of influence.
- Relationship to existing blueprint documents.
- Long-term maintenance implications.
- Degree of organizational visibility.
- Potential need for future review.

Classification should never be determined solely by implementation effort or project duration.

A technically simple change may represent a strategically significant Product Experience decision, while a technically complex implementation may simply execute an already documented decision.

---

## Classification Is Dynamic

The classification assigned to a decision is not necessarily permanent.

As the product evolves, the influence of a decision may expand or diminish.

Governance should therefore allow classification to be revisited whenever the broader Product Experience context changes.

Reclassification should preserve historical continuity while ensuring that governance efforts remain aligned with the current strategic importance of each decision.

---

By distinguishing decisions according to their organizational significance rather than implementation complexity, the Design Decision framework enables Omon to apply governance consistently while maintaining the flexibility required for continuous product evolution.
# 9. Decision Evaluation Framework

Effective design decisions are not determined by confidence alone.

They are evaluated through a structured framework that promotes objective reasoning, alignment with the Product Experience Blueprint, and long-term product quality.

The purpose of evaluation is not to prove that a decision is correct.

Instead, evaluation seeks to determine whether sufficient reasoning exists to justify the selected direction based on the information available at the time.

The evaluation framework provides a consistent approach for assessing decisions regardless of their scope, complexity, or organizational visibility.

---

## Evaluation Objectives

Every evaluation should contribute to one or more of the following objectives:

- Improve Product Experience quality.
- Preserve long-term consistency.
- Reduce unnecessary subjectivity.
- Strengthen documented reasoning.
- Encourage evidence-informed discussions.
- Improve future maintainability.
- Support responsible product evolution.

Evaluation exists to improve decision quality rather than to create approval barriers.

---

## Evaluation Dimensions

Every strategic design decision should be considered from multiple complementary perspectives.

No single dimension should dominate the overall evaluation.

### User Value

Does the decision improve the overall user experience?

Evaluation should consider whether the proposed direction supports user understanding, confidence, efficiency, learnability, accessibility, and overall satisfaction.

The user's experience remains the primary consideration.

---

### Product Alignment

Does the decision reinforce the Product Positioning, Brand Identity, Design Philosophy, Design Language, and Product Experience Blueprint?

A decision that solves a localized problem while weakening overall product identity should be reconsidered.

Alignment is evaluated across the entire Product Experience rather than within an isolated interface.

---

### Consistency

Does the decision maintain consistency with existing design principles?

Consistency includes interaction behavior, terminology, information hierarchy, visual communication, and overall user expectations.

The objective is preserving coherence throughout the product ecosystem.

---

### Sustainability

Will the reasoning remain understandable as the product evolves?

Evaluation should consider the long-term implications of the decision rather than only its immediate benefits.

Sustainable decisions reduce future redesign effort while supporting continuous product growth.

---

### Evidence Quality

Is the documented reasoning supported by appropriate evidence?

Evidence may originate from research, usability evaluation, accessibility reviews, audit findings, organizational learning, or other structured observations.

The objective is ensuring that strategic decisions extend beyond personal preference.

---

### Risk Awareness

Every decision introduces trade-offs.

Evaluation should acknowledge potential risks, known limitations, assumptions, and areas of uncertainty.

Recognizing uncertainty improves future review quality and encourages responsible decision making.

---

## Balanced Evaluation

Evaluation should balance competing considerations instead of optimizing a single objective.

Examples include balancing:

- Simplicity and flexibility.
- Innovation and consistency.
- User expectations and organizational constraints.
- Immediate improvements and long-term maintainability.

A well-balanced decision generally provides greater long-term value than one optimized exclusively for a single criterion.

---

## Evaluation Outcome

The outcome of an evaluation should provide sufficient confidence that the documented reasoning is appropriate for the current Product Experience context.

Evaluation does not guarantee permanent correctness.

Instead, it establishes that the decision represents the most appropriate direction based on available knowledge at the time of evaluation.

Future reviews remain an expected part of responsible Product Experience governance.

---

# 10. Decision Review Process

Every documented decision should remain open to structured review throughout its lifecycle.

Review ensures that previously documented reasoning continues supporting the evolving needs of the product without introducing unnecessary instability.

The objective of review is not to challenge decisions indiscriminately.

Instead, review confirms whether existing reasoning remains valid within the current Product Experience context.

---

## Review Philosophy

Reviews should be deliberate rather than reactive.

A decision should not be revisited simply because preferences have changed.

Instead, meaningful review should be motivated by new information, evolving product requirements, or changes that materially affect the original assumptions.

Structured review protects long-term consistency while allowing responsible evolution.

---

## Review Triggers

A review may become appropriate when circumstances such as the following occur:

- New user insights become available.
- Product strategy evolves.
- Brand direction changes.
- Product Experience principles are revised.
- Accessibility standards change.
- Significant usability findings emerge.
- Existing assumptions are no longer valid.
- Multiple related decisions begin conflicting.
- Long-term product growth introduces new constraints.

The presence of a review trigger does not automatically require changing a decision.

It simply indicates that re-evaluation may be beneficial.

---

## Review Activities

A structured review typically involves:

- Revisiting the original reasoning.
- Validating previous assumptions.
- Assessing current product context.
- Comparing expected and observed outcomes.
- Identifying newly available evidence.
- Determining whether the decision remains appropriate.

The emphasis remains on understanding rather than replacing previous work.

---

## Review Outcomes

A review generally results in one of the following conclusions:

### Retain

The original reasoning continues supporting the intended Product Experience.

No further action is required beyond maintaining the documented decision.

---

### Clarify

The decision remains appropriate, but additional documentation improves future understanding.

Clarification strengthens documentation without changing the decision itself.

---

### Revise

New evidence or changing circumstances justify updating the documented reasoning.

The revised decision supersedes the previous version while preserving historical traceability.

---

### Retire

The decision is no longer applicable because the surrounding Product Experience has fundamentally evolved.

Retirement preserves historical context while indicating that the decision should no longer guide future work.

---

## Review Documentation

Review activities should preserve sufficient context to explain:

- Why the review occurred.
- Which information was reconsidered.
- Whether assumptions changed.
- Which conclusion was reached.
- Whether governance documentation requires updates.

Review documentation maintains continuity across successive generations of Product Experience decisions.

---

## Continuous Learning

Review should never be interpreted as evidence that an earlier decision failed.

Product Experience evolves through continuous learning.

Responsible governance recognizes that the quality of a decision depends on the information available when it was made.

As knowledge grows, documented reasoning should evolve accordingly.

A mature Product Experience is therefore characterized not by the absence of change, but by the disciplined and transparent management of change over time.
# 11. Decision Change Management

Change is an expected characteristic of every successful product.

As Omon Dashboard evolves, previously documented design decisions may require refinement to reflect new knowledge, changing user needs, technological advancement, or strategic product direction.

The objective of change management is not to prevent change.

Its objective is ensuring that change occurs deliberately, transparently, and consistently.

Every modification to a documented decision should preserve the integrity of the Product Experience Blueprint while maintaining historical continuity.

---

## Change Principles

All changes to documented design decisions should follow several fundamental principles.

### Intentional

Changes should always address a clearly understood reason.

A documented decision should never change solely because implementation preferences, temporary trends, or individual opinions have shifted.

Meaningful change begins with meaningful justification.

---

### Transparent

The rationale behind every revision should remain visible.

Future contributors should understand not only that a decision changed, but also why the change became necessary.

Transparency strengthens organizational trust while reducing future ambiguity.

---

### Traceable

Changes should preserve historical relationships between previous and current decisions.

Earlier reasoning remains valuable organizational knowledge even after it has been superseded.

Maintaining historical continuity reduces repeated discussions and protects institutional learning.

---

### Proportional

The governance effort applied to a change should reflect the significance of its impact.

Strategic decisions generally require more comprehensive evaluation than localized operational improvements.

This proportional approach balances governance quality with practical efficiency.

---

### Blueprint-Aligned

Every revised decision should remain consistent with the Product Experience Blueprint.

If a proposed change introduces conflict with established principles, the broader blueprint should be reviewed before the individual decision is updated.

The Product Experience Blueprint remains the primary source of strategic alignment.

---

## Managing Decision Evolution

A documented decision may evolve through:

- Refinement of existing reasoning.
- Updated assumptions.
- Newly available evidence.
- Expanded product scope.
- Organizational learning.
- Product maturity.

Regardless of the reason, the continuity of reasoning should always be preserved.

Evolution should strengthen organizational understanding rather than replace it.

---

# 12. Relationship with Product Blueprint

The Design Decision framework exists to support the Product Experience Blueprint rather than operate independently.

Every documented decision should reinforce the intent established throughout the blueprint and contribute to a consistent long-term Product Experience.

The blueprint defines the strategic direction of the product.

The Design Decision framework governs how future evolution remains aligned with that direction.

## Blueprint as Strategic Foundation

Although closely related, the blueprint and documented decisions serve different purposes.

## Blueprint Evolution

In exceptional circumstances, accumulated design decisions may reveal opportunities to strengthen the Product Experience Blueprint itself.

# 13. Decision Traceability

Design knowledge becomes significantly more valuable when relationships between decisions remain visible over time.

Traceability enables future contributors to understand how Product Experience reasoning has evolved without reconstructing historical context from fragmented documentation.

The objective of traceability is preserving continuity rather than creating administrative complexity.

---

## Traceability Objectives

Decision traceability supports several long-term objectives:

- Preserve organizational knowledge.
- Improve historical understanding.
- Reduce repeated discussions.
- Simplify future reviews.
- Support consistent governance.
- Strengthen cross-document relationships.

Traceability transforms isolated decisions into a connected knowledge system.

---

## Traceability Relationships

A documented decision may relate to:

- Earlier design decisions.
- Subsequent revisions.
- Product Experience principles.
- Brand Identity guidance.
- Design Language guidance.
- Information Architecture guidance.
- Component System guidance.
- Audit findings.
- Future governance reviews.

Understanding these relationships provides richer context than reviewing individual decisions in isolation.

---

## Historical Continuity

Historical documentation should remain available even after decisions have evolved.

Removing previous reasoning weakens organizational learning and increases the likelihood of repeating earlier discussions.

Preserving historical continuity enables future teams to understand not only what changed, but also how Product Experience thinking matured over time.

---

## Organizational Memory

Traceability gradually establishes an institutional memory that extends beyond individual contributors.

As the product grows, documented relationships between decisions become increasingly valuable for onboarding, collaboration, governance, and long-term product stewardship.

Organizational memory should therefore be considered a strategic Product Experience asset rather than merely historical documentation.

---

## Sustainable Knowledge

The ultimate objective of traceability is ensuring that Product Experience knowledge remains understandable regardless of organizational growth, team changes, or future redesign initiatives.

Every documented relationship contributes to a stronger and more resilient governance ecosystem.

Long-term governance seeks to achieve the following objectives:

- Preserve Product Experience consistency.

- Improve decision transparency.
- Support sustainable product evolution.
- Reduce unnecessary design fragmentation.
- Strengthen cross-functional collaboration.
- Maintain alignment with the Product Experience Blueprint.

---

### Adaptable

Adaptability allows the framework to remain relevant without sacrificing its underlying principles.

### Sustainable

Sustainability therefore balances rigor with usability.

### Transparent

Transparent governance improves accountability, collaboration, and organizational trust.

### Knowledge-Oriented

Knowledge preservation is considered an essential outcome of governance rather than a secondary benefit.

# 15. Design Decision Success Criteria

The success of the Design Decision framework should not be measured by the number of documented decisions.

Its effectiveness is determined by the quality, consistency, and long-term usefulness of the documented reasoning.

A successful framework enables future contributors to understand decisions confidently without depending on undocumented historical knowledge.

---

## Characteristics of Successful Design Decisions

A documented decision should be considered successful when it demonstrates the following characteristics.

### Understandable

The purpose, context, and reasoning are clear to future readers without requiring additional explanation.

---

### Aligned

The documented reasoning remains consistent with the Product Experience Blueprint and reinforces Omon's identity as a Calm Financial Companion.

---

### Justified

The selected direction is supported by appropriate reasoning and, where applicable, relevant evidence.

The rationale is sufficiently complete to explain why the decision was made.

---

### Traceable

Relationships with relevant blueprint documents, previous decisions, and future revisions remain understandable throughout the decision lifecycle.

---

### Maintainable

The documentation remains useful as the product evolves.

Future updates can extend the decision without losing historical continuity.

---

### Reviewable

The decision contains enough context to support future evaluation when assumptions, user needs, or product direction change.

---

### Sustainable

The documented reasoning continues providing value beyond the initial implementation.

Long-term usefulness is considered a key indicator of decision quality.

---

- Better Product Experience consistency.

# 16. Definition of Done

A design decision should not be considered complete simply because a conclusion has been reached.

Completion requires that the decision satisfies the governance expectations established throughout this document.

The Definition of Done provides a shared understanding of when a documented decision is sufficiently complete to become part of the Product Experience governance system.

---

## A Design Decision is considered Done when:

- The problem requiring a decision has been clearly identified.
- Relevant context has been sufficiently understood.
- Appropriate alternatives have been considered.
- The selected direction is supported by documented reasoning.
- Assumptions have been identified where appropriate.
- Expected outcomes have been articulated.
- The decision aligns with the Product Experience Blueprint.
- Documentation is clear, understandable, and maintainable.
- The decision can be reviewed in the future.
- Traceability has been preserved.
- Ownership has been established.
- The decision satisfies the governance principles defined throughout this document.

Meeting these criteria indicates that the decision is ready to guide future Product Experience work.

It does not imply that the decision will never change.

Instead, it confirms that the decision has been documented with sufficient quality, transparency, and organizational context to support responsible future evolution.
