# Purpose

This document defines the philosophy, principles, governance, and usage guidelines for every visual asset within Omon.

Rather than prescribing specific visual implementations, this document establishes a consistent decision-making framework that ensures every icon, illustration, graphic, chart, avatar, supporting visual, and decorative element contributes to a unified Product Experience.

The objective is to ensure that visual assets never become isolated decorative objects but instead function as meaningful communication tools that reinforce clarity, trust, emotional comfort, and product consistency.

This document serves as the official reference for maintaining visual coherence throughout the evolution of Omon.

# 1. Introduction

Visual assets are one of the most influential components of a digital product because they communicate meaning long before users begin reading text.

Within Omon, visual assets exist to support understanding rather than decoration.

Every icon, illustration, graph, visual indicator, supporting image, avatar, and graphical element should reduce cognitive effort while reinforcing confidence in the product.

Visual assets are therefore considered part of the overall interaction language instead of independent artistic elements.

This philosophy aligns with Omon's positioning as a Calm Financial Companion.

Financial products often expose users to information that can be dense, numerical, and emotionally sensitive.

A carefully governed visual language helps transform complex financial information into experiences that feel approachable, trustworthy, and understandable.

Visual assets should always strengthen the relationship between users and the product by making interactions easier to recognize, easier to interpret, and easier to remember.

Throughout the entire product journey—from Landing Page through Dashboard—users should experience visual continuity.

They should never feel that different sections of the application belong to different products.

Consistency in visual communication reduces unnecessary learning, improves navigation, strengthens recognition, and reinforces confidence.

The purpose of visual assets is therefore not to impress users with artistic complexity.

Their primary responsibility is to communicate information with clarity, reinforce interaction patterns, reduce ambiguity, and support calm decision making.

Visual assets should behave as silent guides.

They provide context without demanding attention.

They explain without overwhelming.

They support rather than dominate.

Because visual assets become deeply embedded within everyday product interactions, their quality should be evaluated not by how attractive they appear individually, but by how effectively they contribute to the overall Product Experience.

A successful visual asset is one that users naturally understand without consciously noticing its presence.

For this reason, visual assets must always be evaluated as part of the entire experience rather than isolated design components.

Every decision regarding visual communication should ultimately strengthen Omon's identity as a product that feels friendly, calm, helpful, personal, and trustworthy.

# 2. Visual Asset Philosophy

Visual assets are an integral part of Omon's communication system.

They are not independent decorative elements, nor are they intended to increase visual complexity.

Every visual asset exists because it serves a communication purpose that improves the Product Experience.

Within Omon, visual assets help transform financial information into experiences that feel understandable, approachable, and emotionally comfortable.

Rather than attracting attention to themselves, visual assets should direct attention toward user goals.

Users should never need to interpret visual elements as puzzles.

Instead, visual communication should reduce uncertainty by reinforcing meaning already established through information architecture, interaction patterns, and content strategy.

Visual assets therefore act as supporting communicators rather than primary storytellers.

The product should remain understandable even without excessive visual decoration.

When visual assets are introduced, they should increase comprehension rather than replace clear communication.

Every visual asset should contribute to one or more of the following objectives:

- Clarify information.
- Reinforce meaning.
- Support recognition.
- Improve navigation.
- Reduce ambiguity.
- Increase confidence.
- Create emotional comfort.
- Strengthen product identity.

If a visual element contributes to none of these objectives, its presence should be reconsidered.

Visual simplicity is preferred over visual abundance.

The philosophy favors intentional communication instead of visual richness.

Every additional visual element introduces cognitive cost.

Therefore, visual assets should justify their existence through measurable improvements to user understanding.

Consistency is more valuable than novelty.

A familiar visual system allows users to build long-term recognition and predictable expectations throughout the product.

Visual assets should therefore evolve gradually rather than changing dramatically between product releases.

Because Omon is positioned as a Calm Financial Companion, visual assets should encourage calm interpretation of financial information.

They should never create unnecessary urgency, distraction, or emotional pressure.

Visual communication should remain respectful of the user's attention.

Rather than demanding focus, visual assets should quietly support decision making.

The product succeeds when users feel guided rather than instructed.

Visual assets therefore become companions that help users understand their financial situation instead of overwhelming them with visual complexity.

Ultimately, every visual asset should strengthen one unified perception:

Omon is a trustworthy product that communicates financial information with clarity, consistency, and empathy.

# 3. Relationship Between Visual Assets and Product Experience

Visual assets should never exist independently from the overall Product Experience.

They are one layer within a larger communication ecosystem that also includes language, interaction, navigation, hierarchy, feedback, and information architecture.

Each of these layers reinforces the others.

When visual assets align with every other experience layer, users perceive the product as coherent.

When they conflict, users experience inconsistency even if individual assets appear visually attractive.

For this reason, visual assets must always be evaluated within the complete interaction context.

An icon does not communicate by itself.

Its surrounding label, placement, spacing, interaction behavior, and information hierarchy collectively determine its meaning.

Likewise, an illustration cannot compensate for unclear workflows.

Charts cannot compensate for poor data organization.

Decorative graphics cannot compensate for weak content.

Visual assets should therefore amplify existing clarity rather than compensate for missing clarity.

Within Omon, every product experience stage has different communication needs.

During onboarding, visual assets primarily reduce uncertainty and create familiarity.

During authentication, visual assets reinforce trust and professionalism.

Within the Dashboard, visual assets help users recognize information structures more efficiently.

Analytics relies on visual assets to communicate relationships between financial data without increasing cognitive effort.

Budget management uses visual assets to reinforce planning and progress while avoiding emotional pressure.

Search experiences rely on visual cues that improve discoverability and orientation.

Import workflows benefit from visuals that increase confidence during review and confirmation processes.

Settings prioritize recognition, predictability, and organizational clarity.

Although these contexts differ, users should always recognize them as belonging to the same product.

Visual continuity across every screen is therefore more important than maximizing visual uniqueness for individual features.

Users should experience one cohesive visual language rather than multiple independent visual styles.

Visual assets also play an important role in emotional continuity.

Financial applications frequently present situations involving uncertainty, mistakes, incomplete information, or unexpected outcomes.

During these moments, visual communication should help reduce anxiety instead of amplifying it.

Calm visual support encourages users to remain engaged while resolving problems.

Visual assets should communicate reassurance before emphasizing action.

They should help users understand what is happening before suggesting what to do next.

This relationship between emotion and information is fundamental to the Omon experience.

Visual assets should reinforce trust through predictability.

Users who repeatedly encounter consistent visual communication gradually build confidence in both the interface and the underlying product.

This accumulated trust becomes more valuable over time than individual visual creativity.

Every visual decision should therefore answer a simple question:

Does this asset make the overall Product Experience feel more understandable, more consistent, and more trustworthy?

If the answer is unclear, the asset should be reconsidered before becoming part of the product.

# 4. Visual Communication Principles

Visual communication within Omon should always prioritize understanding over expression.

Every visual decision should help users complete their objectives with greater confidence and less cognitive effort.

Visual assets are successful when users understand information naturally without consciously interpreting the visuals themselves.

To achieve this objective, all visual communication should follow several fundamental principles.

## Purpose Before Appearance

Every visual element should exist because it communicates something meaningful.

Visual attractiveness alone is never sufficient justification for introducing additional visual elements.

Before introducing any visual asset, designers should clearly understand:

- What information it communicates.
- Why visual communication is necessary.
- How it improves user understanding.
- What problem it solves.
- Whether simpler alternatives already exist.

Purpose should always precede aesthetics.

## Clarity Before Complexity

Visual communication should simplify information rather than increase interpretation effort.

Whenever multiple visual approaches are possible, preference should be given to the solution that users can understand most quickly.

Complex visual expression should never become an obstacle to comprehension.

Users should spend their attention understanding financial information—not deciphering interface visuals.

## Consistency Before Creativity

Creativity is valuable only when it strengthens consistency.

Introducing visually unique assets that differ from the rest of the product may create short-term interest but often reduces long-term familiarity.

Omon values recognizable patterns over isolated moments of originality.

Users should gradually develop visual intuition throughout repeated product usage.

## Reinforcement Instead of Replacement

Visual assets should reinforce information rather than replace it.

Meaning should never depend entirely on a visual element.

Users with different levels of experience, perception, or accessibility needs should still understand the interface through multiple communication channels.

Visual communication should therefore complement structure, language, hierarchy, and interaction.

## Calm Visual Rhythm

Financial products often expose users to large amounts of information.

Visual communication should organize attention instead of competing for attention.

Strong emphasis should remain limited to information that genuinely requires prioritization.

Everything else should contribute to a calm visual rhythm that supports focused decision making.

## Progressive Communication

Not every visual asset needs equal prominence.

The interface should reveal visual emphasis progressively according to user context.

Primary tasks deserve stronger visual support.

Secondary information should remain available without distracting from immediate objectives.

Visual hierarchy should evolve naturally throughout each workflow.

## Familiar Recognition

Whenever users encounter similar concepts across different sections of the application, visual communication should reinforce recognition instead of introducing alternative representations.

Repeated exposure to consistent visual language reduces learning effort and increases confidence.

Recognition should always be easier than recollection.

## Emotional Stability

Visual communication should avoid unintentionally increasing emotional pressure.

Even when presenting financial risks, budget limitations, failed imports, or incomplete data, visuals should remain supportive rather than alarming.

The goal is to help users recover, understand, and continue—not to amplify stress.

# 5. Iconography Principles

Icons are one of the most frequently encountered visual assets throughout the Omon experience.

Because of their repeated use, even small inconsistencies can significantly affect users' perception of product quality and coherence.

Icons should therefore function as a unified communication system rather than independent graphical symbols.

Their primary responsibility is recognition.

Their secondary responsibility is efficiency.

Decoration is never their primary purpose.

## Icons Represent Meaning

Every icon should represent a concept that users can reasonably recognize.

Icons should communicate familiar ideas instead of abstract artistic interpretations.

If users cannot reasonably predict the meaning of an icon without explanation, the icon should be reconsidered.

Meaning should feel intuitive through repeated product usage.

## Icons Support Labels

Icons should reinforce textual communication rather than replace it.

Whenever users benefit from explicit language, icons should accompany text instead of acting as the sole communication method.

The relationship between icon and label should improve recognition through redundancy rather than dependency.

## Predictability

Similar actions should always be represented consistently throughout the product.

Users should never need to learn multiple visual representations for the same concept.

Likewise, different concepts should not share visually ambiguous representations that increase interpretation effort.

Consistency strengthens recognition over time.

## Functional Neutrality

Icons should communicate function rather than emotion.

Emotional interpretation should arise from the overall experience instead of individual iconography.

Icons should therefore remain visually neutral, allowing surrounding context to define urgency, importance, or tone.

## Context Determines Meaning

An icon rarely communicates meaning independently.

Its surrounding content, placement, grouping, interaction behavior, and associated text collectively establish user understanding.

Icons should therefore always be evaluated within their complete interaction context rather than as isolated graphical objects.

## Recognition Across Features

Users should recognize familiar icon usage regardless of which product area they are currently using.

Whether interacting with Dashboard, Analytics, Budget, Search, Import, or Settings, the relationship between icons and user expectations should remain stable.

Consistency across features reinforces confidence in navigation and interaction.

## Appropriate Visual Weight

Icons should support interface hierarchy without dominating it.

They should help organize information while allowing content and interaction to remain the primary focus.

Visual emphasis should arise from importance rather than graphical size or complexity.

## Avoid Redundant Decoration

Icons should not be added solely to make an interface appear more visually rich.

When an icon does not improve recognition, orientation, or understanding, it should generally be omitted.

Reducing unnecessary icon usage often improves overall clarity.

## Long-Term Scalability

The icon system should remain sustainable as Omon grows.

New product capabilities should extend the existing visual language rather than introducing isolated icon behaviors.

Expansion should feel evolutionary instead of fragmented.

A scalable icon philosophy allows users to carry prior knowledge into future product experiences with minimal additional learning.

# 6. Illustration Principles

Illustrations provide emotional context that complements functional interactions.

Unlike icons, which primarily improve recognition, illustrations help establish atmosphere, reinforce understanding, and support user confidence during specific moments of the product journey.

Within Omon, illustrations should never become the primary source of information.

Their role is to support communication that has already been established through content, interaction, and information hierarchy.

Illustrations should reduce emotional distance between users and financial management.

Financial topics can often appear intimidating, technical, or overwhelming.

Illustrations help present these experiences in a manner that feels approachable without diminishing their importance.

## Purposeful Communication

Illustrations should always communicate a meaningful purpose.

Their presence should contribute to one or more of the following objectives:

- Introduce context.
- Explain situations.
- Reinforce understanding.
- Encourage confidence.
- Reduce uncertainty.
- Create emotional warmth.
- Celebrate meaningful progress.
- Humanize digital interactions.

Illustrations that serve no communicative purpose should not be introduced simply to occupy empty space.

## Supporting Rather Than Leading

Illustrations should complement the surrounding interface rather than dominate it.

Users should naturally focus on completing their tasks while illustrations quietly reinforce the emotional quality of the experience.

Illustrations should never compete with primary actions, essential information, or workflow progression.

The interface should remain functional even when illustrations are absent.

## Emotional Moderation

Illustrations should promote emotional stability.

Visual storytelling should avoid exaggerating success, failure, urgency, or disappointment.

Financial management is a continuous journey rather than a sequence of dramatic events.

Illustrations should therefore encourage sustainable engagement instead of emotional extremes.

## Contextual Relevance

Illustrations should align with the purpose of the current screen.

Each illustration should strengthen users' understanding of the situation they are experiencing rather than introducing unrelated visual narratives.

Users should immediately perceive a meaningful relationship between the illustration and the surrounding interface.

Contextual relevance strengthens comprehension and reduces distraction.

## Consistent Product Identity

Although illustrations may appear across different product areas, they should collectively reinforce one unified product personality.

Users should never feel that illustrations belong to different visual ecosystems.

Consistency should emerge through shared communication principles rather than repetitive visual motifs.

Illustrations should always contribute to Omon's identity as a calm, friendly, helpful, personal, and trustworthy financial companion.

## Respecting User Attention

Illustrations should acknowledge that users visit Omon primarily to manage finances.

Visual storytelling should therefore remain proportional to the importance of the task.

Interfaces supporting frequent or repetitive activities should prioritize efficiency over visual expression.

Illustrations should enhance memorable moments without slowing everyday interactions.

## Long-Term Evolution

Illustration usage should remain flexible enough to support future product growth.

As Omon expands into new features and workflows, new illustrations should naturally extend the existing communication philosophy.

Growth should strengthen the overall experience rather than introducing disconnected visual narratives.

# 7. Photography Principles

Photography represents the most realistic form of visual communication available within the product.

Because photographs naturally carry strong emotional and contextual associations, they should be used with careful consideration.

Within Omon, photography should always support trust, authenticity, and clarity.

It should never distract from financial information or create unrealistic expectations.

## Authentic Communication

Photography should reinforce genuine human experiences.

Users should perceive photographs as supporting credibility rather than functioning as promotional decoration.

Authenticity builds confidence more effectively than visual perfection.

Whenever photography is introduced, it should strengthen the relationship between users and the product rather than shifting attention away from user goals.

## Context Before Emotion

Photographs should first communicate relevant context.

Emotional expression should emerge naturally from meaningful situations rather than exaggerated visual storytelling.

Images that create unnecessary emotional intensity should be avoided.

Financial management benefits from reassurance, not emotional pressure.

## Supporting Information

Photography should never become the primary carrier of product information.

Important messages should remain understandable through structure, content, and interaction.

Photographs should provide supporting context without replacing explicit communication.

Users should never depend on visual interpretation alone to understand essential information.

## Trust Through Realism

Photography should strengthen users' perception that Omon understands everyday financial life.

Visual communication should feel relatable and grounded rather than aspirational or idealized.

The product should respect diverse financial journeys without suggesting that success has a single visual representation.

## Appropriate Usage Context

Photography should be reserved for situations where real-world representation genuinely improves understanding or emotional connection.

Routine operational workflows typically benefit more from interface clarity than from additional photographic content.

Product decisions should therefore prioritize necessity over visual richness.

## Visual Harmony

Whenever photography appears alongside other visual assets, it should integrate naturally into the surrounding communication system.

Users should perceive photography, illustrations, icons, charts, and interface components as parts of one unified experience rather than separate visual languages.

Consistency of communication remains more important than consistency of artistic style.

## Longevity

Photography should contribute to a timeless product experience.

Visual communication should remain relevant as user expectations, financial habits, and product capabilities evolve.

Preference should always be given to enduring communication principles over visuals that depend on temporary trends or short-lived aesthetics.

# 8. Data Visualization Principles

Financial information is most valuable when users can understand it quickly and confidently.

Data visualization exists to transform numerical information into meaningful insights without increasing cognitive effort.

Within Omon, charts, graphs, indicators, visual summaries, and other data-driven visual assets should always prioritize comprehension over visual sophistication.

The objective is not to impress users with complex graphics, but to help them make better financial decisions.

## Data Before Decoration

Visualizations should communicate information before expressing visual identity.

Every graphical element should correspond to meaningful information.

Decorative elements that do not improve interpretation should be minimized.

Users should never question whether a visual element represents actual data or merely visual embellishment.

## Reveal Relationships

Visualizations should emphasize relationships rather than isolated values.

Users benefit most when they can naturally understand:

- Changes over time.
- Distribution of spending.
- Comparisons between categories.
- Budget progress.
- Financial balance.
- Trends.
- Patterns.
- Exceptions.

The visualization should guide attention toward meaningful insights rather than requiring manual interpretation.

## Support Decision Making

Every visualization should help users answer practical questions.

Examples include:

- What changed?
- Where did money go?
- Is spending increasing?
- Am I within budget?
- Which category requires attention?
- How is this month different from previous periods?

If a visualization cannot support meaningful decisions, its necessity should be reconsidered.

## Progressive Complexity

Not every user requires the same level of analytical depth.

Visualizations should present information progressively.

Users should first understand the overall situation before exploring detailed analysis.

Overview should precede exploration.

Summary should precede detail.

Pattern should precede precision.

This progressive approach reduces cognitive overload while supporting users with varying levels of financial literacy.

## Calm Interpretation

Financial information may contain both positive and negative outcomes.

Visualizations should communicate these outcomes objectively.

The interface should avoid creating unnecessary emotional reactions through exaggerated visual emphasis.

Users should feel informed rather than judged.

Calm interpretation encourages better long-term financial decision making.

## Consistency Across Product Areas

The same financial concepts should always be communicated consistently regardless of where users encounter them.

Income, expenses, savings, budgets, trends, and comparisons should maintain recognizable visual relationships throughout the product.

Consistency reduces relearning and strengthens user confidence.

## Encourage Exploration

Well-designed visualizations invite curiosity without creating confusion.

Users should feel encouraged to explore their financial information naturally.

The product should reward exploration by revealing increasingly meaningful insights while maintaining clarity at every level.

Visualization should therefore function as an extension of conversation rather than a replacement for understanding.

# 9. Empty State Illustration Principles

An empty state represents the absence of expected content.

Rather than being viewed as incomplete screens, empty states should be considered intentional moments within the overall Product Experience.

They communicate system status, guide user expectations, and encourage the next meaningful action.

Within Omon, empty states should reduce uncertainty while maintaining a calm and supportive atmosphere.

## Communicate the Situation Clearly

Users should immediately understand why information is unavailable.

The interface should communicate the current situation transparently without requiring users to infer system behavior.

Empty states should answer fundamental questions such as:

- Why is nothing shown?
- Is this expected?
- Has something failed?
- What can I do next?

Visual assets should reinforce these answers without introducing ambiguity.

## Encourage Progress

Empty states should motivate users toward meaningful next steps.

Rather than emphasizing the absence of content, visual communication should gently support continued progress.

Users should feel invited to continue their journey instead of feeling blocked by missing information.

Encouragement should remain calm, respectful, and free from unnecessary urgency.

## Reduce Perceived Failure

An empty screen does not necessarily indicate an error.

Many empty states simply reflect the natural beginning of a user's financial journey.

Visual assets should therefore distinguish between:

- Initial product setup.
- Temporary absence of data.
- Successful completion with no results.
- Filtered views without matches.
- System limitations.
- Unexpected problems.

Helping users understand these differences reduces confusion and improves confidence.

## Maintain Emotional Comfort

Managing personal finances can already introduce uncertainty.

Empty states should avoid amplifying that uncertainty through alarming visuals or discouraging messaging.

Instead, visual communication should reassure users that the product remains functional and ready to support them whenever information becomes available.

## Preserve Interface Continuity

Empty states should feel like a natural extension of the surrounding interface.

Users should recognize them as temporary conditions within an ongoing workflow rather than separate experiences.

The transition between populated and empty interfaces should feel seamless.

Visual continuity reinforces trust in system behavior.

## Action Should Follow Understanding

Users should first understand the current situation before being encouraged to take action.

Visual assets should support comprehension before directing behavior.

When actions are presented, they should feel like logical next steps arising from the user's current context rather than generic calls to action.

## Consistency Across Product Areas

Every product area experiences empty states differently.

Dashboard, Analytics, Budget, Search, Import, and Settings each present unique situations.

Despite these contextual differences, users should experience one coherent communication philosophy.

Visual assets should consistently reinforce clarity, confidence, and calm progression regardless of feature area.

## Celebrate New Beginnings

The first interaction with an empty product represents an important opportunity.

Rather than highlighting what users lack, empty states should emphasize the potential value that awaits as users continue building their financial history.

Visual communication should encourage optimism without making unrealistic promises.

Users should feel that they are beginning a meaningful journey with a trusted financial companion.

# 10. Avatar and Identity Representation

Identity representation helps users recognize people, ownership, collaboration, and personalization throughout the product.

Whether representing an individual user, a shared workspace, or other identifiable entities, visual identity should primarily improve recognition rather than provide decoration.

Within Omon, identity representation should strengthen users' understanding of who or what they are interacting with.

## Recognition Before Personalization

Identity visuals should first improve recognition.

Personalization is valuable only when it strengthens users' ability to distinguish people, workspaces, or contextual entities.

Visual identity should reduce confusion rather than introduce unnecessary individuality.

## Respect User Context

Identity representation should always reflect the context in which users are working.

Users should never need to question whose information they are viewing or which workspace they are currently managing.

Visual identity should quietly reinforce contextual awareness throughout the experience.

## Consistency Across Product Areas

Identity representation should remain recognizable regardless of where it appears.

Users should experience continuous recognition when moving between Dashboard, Analytics, Budget, Search, Import, Settings, and other future product areas.

Consistency strengthens orientation while reducing cognitive effort.

## Support Trust

Identity-related visuals should reinforce confidence when users perform important financial activities.

Users should immediately recognize ownership, participation, and responsibility without relying exclusively on textual confirmation.

Visual support should strengthen—not replace—clear product communication.

## Avoid Visual Dominance

Identity representation should remain supportive.

Users interact with financial information rather than identity assets.

Identity visuals should therefore contribute to orientation without becoming the primary visual focus of the interface.

## Future Adaptability

Identity representation should remain flexible enough to accommodate future collaboration models, multiple workspaces, expanded account structures, and evolving personalization capabilities.

Future expansion should preserve recognition while maintaining consistency with the overall Product Experience.

# 11. Decorative Elements

Decorative elements contribute atmosphere rather than information.

Within Omon, decoration should never compete with functional communication.

Its purpose is to strengthen emotional quality while preserving interface clarity.

Decoration should always remain secondary to user goals.

## Function Through Atmosphere

Decorative elements should improve how the interface feels rather than how it functions.

They may reinforce product personality, visual rhythm, or emotional comfort, but they should never become required for understanding product behavior.

The interface should remain fully understandable without decorative enhancement.

## Visual Restraint

Financial applications benefit from disciplined visual composition.

Decoration should therefore be introduced carefully and intentionally.

Every additional decorative element increases visual density.

Whenever uncertainty exists, simpler compositions should be preferred.

Restraint supports long-term usability more effectively than excessive visual richness.

## Support Product Personality

Decorative elements should reinforce Omon's brand personality:

- Friendly
- Calm
- Helpful
- Personal
- Trustworthy

Decoration should never contradict these qualities by introducing visual noise, unnecessary excitement, or inconsistent emotional tone.

Every decorative decision should strengthen the perception of Omon as a reliable financial companion.

## Contextual Placement

Decoration should respect the importance of surrounding information.

Areas requiring concentration—such as reviewing transactions, managing budgets, interpreting analytics, or confirming imports—should prioritize functional clarity over decorative expression.

Conversely, moments that introduce, welcome, celebrate, or gently guide users may benefit from subtle decorative support.

The level of decoration should therefore adapt to user context while remaining consistent with the overall philosophy.

## Longevity

Decorative elements should remain relevant as the product evolves.

Design decisions based solely on temporary trends risk creating visual inconsistency over time.

Preference should be given to enduring communication principles that continue supporting the Product Experience across future releases.

# 12. Visual Consistency Principles

Consistency is the foundation of user familiarity.

Users build confidence through repeated exposure to predictable visual communication.

Every interaction reinforces expectations that influence future interactions.

Visual consistency therefore extends beyond appearance.

It reflects the product's reliability, professionalism, and respect for users' attention.

## One Product, One Visual Language

Regardless of feature area, users should always perceive a single visual identity.

Landing Page, Register, Login, Dashboard, Analytics, Budget, Search, Import, and Settings should feel like different chapters of the same product rather than independent applications.

Visual assets should reinforce this continuity throughout every stage of the user journey.

## Consistency Across Time

Users should not need to relearn visual communication after routine product updates.

While the product should continue evolving, changes should occur progressively.

Evolution should strengthen familiarity instead of disrupting established mental models.

## Consistency Across Context

Similar situations should communicate similar meanings.

Equivalent concepts should receive equivalent visual treatment regardless of where they appear.

Consistency allows users to transfer knowledge from one workflow to another with minimal additional learning.

## Consistency Across Communication Channels

Visual assets should align naturally with language, interaction patterns, navigation, hierarchy, and content.

No communication layer should contradict another.

When every communication layer reinforces the same message, users experience the product as coherent and trustworthy.

## Consistency Across User Experience Levels

New users and experienced users should both benefit from the same visual system.

Beginners should quickly develop understanding through predictable communication.

Experienced users should continue benefiting from accumulated familiarity without encountering unnecessary visual variation.

## Consistency Supports Trust

Trust grows through repeated positive experiences.

Every consistent interaction strengthens users' confidence that future interactions will behave predictably.

Visual consistency therefore becomes more than an aesthetic objective.

It becomes an essential contributor to product reliability.

Every visual decision should strengthen users' confidence that Omon communicates clearly today and will continue doing so tomorrow.

# 13. Accessibility Considerations

Accessibility is a fundamental quality of good visual communication.

Visual assets should improve understanding for the broadest possible range of users without creating unnecessary barriers.

Within Omon, accessibility is not treated as an optional enhancement but as an essential characteristic of every visual communication decision.

Accessible visual assets increase clarity for all users, not only for those with permanent accessibility needs.

## Multiple Communication Channels

Important information should never rely on visual assets alone.

Users should be able to understand essential messages through a combination of visual structure, textual communication, interaction patterns, and contextual information.

Visual assets should reinforce understanding rather than become the only source of meaning.

## Clear Recognition

Visual elements should remain distinguishable within their intended context.

Recognition should arise from consistent communication rather than from subtle visual differences that may not be perceived equally by all users.

The objective is reliable interpretation instead of visual complexity.

## Predictable Behavior

Users should develop confidence through repeated exposure to familiar visual communication.

Visual assets should behave consistently across workflows, allowing users to transfer previously learned understanding into new situations.

Predictability improves usability for users with varying levels of experience and diverse cognitive preferences.

## Support Different Levels of Financial Literacy

Users arrive with different backgrounds and varying familiarity with financial concepts.

Visual communication should reduce interpretation effort regardless of users' financial knowledge.

The interface should help users understand financial information without assuming prior expertise.

## Respect Attention

Accessibility also includes respecting users' limited attention.

Visual assets should avoid unnecessary competition for focus.

Interfaces should guide attention intentionally while allowing users to remain in control of their own reading and decision-making pace.

## Inclusive Communication

Visual assets should support inclusive product experiences.

They should communicate with clarity, neutrality, and respect for diverse users and diverse financial situations.

Product communication should remain welcoming without assuming a specific lifestyle, experience level, or personal circumstance.

# 14. Visual Asset Governance

Visual consistency can only be maintained when every visual decision follows a shared governance framework.

Governance provides continuity as products evolve, teams grow, and new features are introduced.

Rather than restricting creativity, governance ensures that creativity contributes to one coherent Product Experience.

## Shared Decision Making

Visual asset decisions should be evaluated against the broader Product Experience rather than individual feature preferences.

Every addition or modification should strengthen the product as a whole.

Short-term convenience should never compromise long-term consistency.

## Alignment With Product Principles

New visual assets should always remain aligned with the design philosophy, brand identity, design language, information architecture, component system, and content strategy established within the Product Experience Blueprint.

Visual governance exists to preserve this alignment across every stage of product evolution.

## Intentional Introduction

New categories of visual assets should only be introduced when they solve meaningful communication problems.

Expansion should result from genuine product needs rather than temporary trends or isolated preferences.

Intentional growth reduces fragmentation and preserves visual coherence.

## Continuous Evaluation

Visual assets should be reviewed periodically as the product evolves.

Evaluation should consider:

- Communication effectiveness.
- Consistency.
- User comprehension.
- Emotional alignment.
- Product coherence.
- Long-term sustainability.

Governance is an ongoing practice rather than a one-time approval process.

## Cross-Functional Responsibility

Maintaining visual quality is a shared responsibility.

Design, Product, Engineering, Quality Assurance, Documentation, and other contributing teams should all evaluate visual decisions through the same guiding principles.

Shared ownership strengthens consistency throughout the product lifecycle.

# 15. Future Scalability

Omon is expected to evolve continuously.

Its visual communication system should therefore support sustainable growth without sacrificing clarity or consistency.

Scalability is achieved not through rigid rules but through enduring principles that remain applicable as the product expands.

## Extend Rather Than Replace

Future visual assets should extend the existing communication system.

Users should recognize continuity even as new capabilities are introduced.

Growth should feel evolutionary rather than disruptive.

## Support Expanding Product Scope

Future modules, workflows, integrations, and user scenarios should naturally fit within the established visual philosophy.

The communication framework should remain applicable regardless of product size or functional complexity.

## Preserve Familiarity

As users continue investing time in Omon, accumulated familiarity becomes increasingly valuable.

Future expansion should protect this familiarity by reinforcing recognizable visual behaviors instead of introducing unnecessary variation.

## Encourage Sustainable Decisions

Scalable visual systems prioritize decisions that remain effective over time.

Preference should be given to communication approaches that continue supporting users as product capabilities evolve.

Long-term sustainability is more valuable than short-lived visual novelty.

# 16. Visual Asset Success Criteria

The Visual Asset Guideline is considered successful when visual communication consistently strengthens the overall Product Experience.

Success should be evaluated through user understanding rather than visual appearance alone.

The following outcomes indicate successful application of this guideline:

- Users recognize common visual patterns without additional explanation.
- Visual assets consistently reinforce product communication.
- Information becomes easier to understand through visual support.
- Different product areas feel like one unified experience.
- Visual communication reduces cognitive effort.
- Users develop confidence through predictable visual interactions.
- Emotional tone remains calm, supportive, and trustworthy.
- Visual decisions remain consistent as the product evolves.
- New visual assets integrate naturally into the existing communication system.
- Product identity becomes increasingly recognizable through repeated use.

Ultimately, successful visual assets become almost invisible.

Users remember how easy the product was to understand rather than the individual visual elements themselves.

# 17. Definition of Done

A visual asset should be considered complete only when it contributes positively to the overall Product Experience.

Completion is determined by communication quality rather than production readiness.

A visual asset satisfies this guideline when:

- It has a clearly defined communication purpose.
- It supports user understanding.
- It aligns with Omon's Product Experience philosophy.
- It reinforces Brand Identity and Design Language.
- It remains consistent with the Information Architecture and Component System.
- It complements Content Strategy rather than replacing it.
- It contributes to a calm and trustworthy experience.
- It avoids unnecessary visual complexity.
- It supports accessibility through clear communication.
- It integrates naturally with existing visual assets.
- It remains adaptable for future product evolution.
- It strengthens the perception of Omon as a single, cohesive product.

A visual asset is not considered finished simply because it is visually appealing.

It is considered finished when it consistently helps users understand, navigate, and trust the product while reinforcing Omon's identity as a Calm Financial Companion.
